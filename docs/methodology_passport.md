# Methodology Passport Card

## Project Title
**The Impact of Generative Text-to-Image Models on Commercial Visual Artists' Paid Opportunities and Professional Autonomy: A Comparative Synthesis of Empirical Evidence (2023–2026)**

## Authors
- Zhanerke Abdumalik
- Zhansaya Baikenova

## Research Design
**Type:** Mixed-methods comparative synthesis.

**Rationale:** The study combines quantitative findings about paid opportunities, compensation, and asset sales with qualitative evidence about illustrators' workflows and creative autonomy. It compares findings from empirical studies published between 2023 and 2026. Since the project synthesizes existing research, it does not experimentally manipulate variables or automatically establish causation.

## Research Questions
- **RQ1:** What changes in paid opportunities, compensation rates, and labor conditions of visual artists have been documented in 2023–2026 literature following the diffusion of text-to-image models?
- **RQ2:** To what extent is platform authorization of generative AI content associated with changes in the production and sales of human-created assets?
- **RQ3:** How does direct client adoption of generative AI transform illustrators' daily tasks and creative autonomy?

## Hypotheses

### RQ1 — Paid Opportunities and Labor Conditions
- **H0:** The evaluated sources do not document statistically significant declines in contract volume or worsening labor conditions associated with AI.
- **H1:** At least two evaluated sources report significant contractions in job postings, declining earnings, or adverse labor shifts associated with AI.

### RQ2 — Human-Created Asset Production and Sales
- **H0:** After platforms authorize AI-generated assets, production or sales revenue of human-created assets does not significantly decline relative to comparable categories.
- **H1:** Platform authorization of AI-generated assets is associated with a significant decline in listings or sales of human-created assets.

### RQ3 — Creative Autonomy and Workflow
- **H0:** Client adoption of AI is not accompanied by substantial restructuring of illustrators' tasks or creative autonomy.
- **H1:** A substantial proportion of illustrators report a shift from original creation toward AI cleanup or inpainting, alongside a marked loss of creative autonomy.

## Variables

| Research Question | Independent Variable (IV) | Dependent Variable (DV) | Control Variables (CV) |
|---|---|---|---|
| RQ1 | Diffusion of generative text-to-image AI across platforms and sectors (2023–2026) | Contract volume, job postings, compensation, career sustainability | Jurisdiction, specialization, employment type, sampling method |
| RQ2 | Platform policy allowing AI-generated asset listings | Human-created asset listings, unit sales, revenue | Platform demand trends, asset category, seasonality, disclosure rules |
| RQ3 | Client use of AI or AI-related client requirements | Workflow composition, time allocation, creative control | Seniority, professional domain, contract arrangement |

## Primary Metric
The primary metric is the reported percentage change in paid opportunities (such as job postings or contracts) before and after AI diffusion or a relevant policy change, where comparable values are available.

Percentage change = ((After − Before) / Before) × 100.

## Guardrail Metrics
- Data completeness: percentage of extracted records containing the required information.
- Comparability: whether studies use sufficiently similar populations, periods, and outcome definitions.
- Evidence type: quantitative and qualitative findings are identified separately.
- Causal interpretation: observational associations are not presented as proven causal effects.
- Processing quality: the analysis script runs successfully and records missing or invalid values.

## Baseline / Control Group
The baseline is the pre-AI or pre-policy-change period reported in each eligible study. Where available, the comparison group consists of similar non-AI asset categories, platforms, or labor-market outcomes not exposed to the same policy change. If a study does not provide a suitable comparison group, its findings will be reported as observational evidence without claiming a causal effect.

## Data Source and Limitations
The project uses empirical studies published between 2023 and 2026. All numerical findings must be extracted from verified sources. Synthetic sample data in the repository are used only to test the analysis pipeline and must not be treated as empirical evidence.

The availability and comparability of published data may limit the number of outcomes that can be quantitatively synthesized. Qualitative evidence about creative autonomy will be analyzed thematically rather than converted into unsupported numerical estimates.
